    Who was there

    The record sees a lot of who was with you: the people on a calendar entry, the voices of a recording, the faces your photo library names. It sees none of it for certain. A face is a library's guess; an invitee may not have come. Who was there is how you answer once for a whole stretch, instead of once a stay, and how you tell the record the two things only you know: who lives with you, and where your people live. Built from RFC 0043 §11 (Who was there) and §12 (Lives with, on a journey day), as commands and readers; no page yet.

    Every answer is one note/v1 line in your words, tier 2, source manual, appended through the one note path like the other acts. Nothing is written unless you say so, and nothing is fetched.

    With, across a stretch

    $ logbook with --from 2026-06-08 --to 2026-06-12 Ola
#5612 with Ola Nordmann  2026-06-08 13:00
#5613 with Ola Nordmann  2026-06-09 13:00
#5614 with Ola Nordmann  2026-06-10 04:00
#5615 with Ola Nordmann  2026-06-11 13:00
#5616 with Ola Nordmann  2026-06-12 13:00

    One with note per person per day, the same line logbook with DAY NAME writes (the same function, presence.state), at the clock of that day's longest stay: the middle of the part of it that falls in the day's waking hours, so the note lies inside the stay and the Day, people and the journeys read the person there.

    Which hours count: the waking hours of a day run from your day_start (in logbook.json, the clock your day begins at) on its date to local midnight after it. With "day_start": "06:00" the night after a day belongs to the day until 06:00 next morning, but its hours after midnight never count towards the longest stay, so that night never wins and no line lands after midnight. With no day_start (the default, "00:00") the waking hours are the whole calendar day, so a night at home from midnight to 08:00 can still be the longest stay, as on the Wednesday above.

    A day with no stay is stated as logbook with DAY states it, at noon (now, for today). A person already stated on a day is not stated again. Every day is written under one hold of the writer lock, and everything is checked before the first line: a stretch longer than 60 days, a day still to come, or a name that is nobody, two people or you is refused in one sentence, and nothing is written.

    Not with

    $ logbook people not-with Kari --from 2026-06-08 --to 2026-06-14
#5617 note  not-with: Kari Nordmann, 2026-06-08 to 2026-06-14

    One note labelled not-with, the person's id and the stretch under extra (person, from, to). The review-with queue stops proposing that person on those days.

    Lives with, lives in

    $ logbook people lives-with Kari --from 2026-01-01
#5618 note  lives-with: Kari Nordmann, from 2026-01-01
$ logbook people lives-with Kari --until 2026-09-30      # ends the standing one
$ logbook people lives-in Per Bergen
#5619 note  lives-in: Per Hansen, Bergen

    A lives-with note (person, from, until when given) means that at a home stay (a stay at a place of kind home in places.json, or near one) that person is with you without asking, so the queue never asks about them there; away from home their faces propose as anyone's do. --until alone closes the stretch that is open, and is refused when none is. A lives-in note (person, city, from) is where they live; the latest one of a person counts.

    Each note is written on the day you say it, and its words are <label>: <name>, …: the with module reads company from a note that says with Ola, and the colon keeps these notes out of it. A reader never parses the words; the label and extra say what a note states. A retracted note stands no more (logbook repair retract).

    The review-with queue

    $ logbook review with --week 2026-W24
who was there, 2026-06-08 – 2026-06-14 (week 2026-W24)
  Kari Nordmann
      faces on 2 photos across 2 days · invited to 'Lunch'
      on Tue 9 Jun, Wed 10 Jun
      --accept 1
  Ola Nordmann
      in the meeting notes of 'Boat plans'
      on Thu 11 Jun
      --accept 2
  answer: --accept N[,N] (every day of it) · --accept N --days D,D · --not NAME

    review with --journey ID (trip:FIRST:LAST, or a day of it), --week YYYY-Www, or --from D1 --to D2 (60 days at most); --json for one object. Per person, on a line of its own, the name; under it the evidence in words and the days it lies on. The evidence is the with module's:

    • faces on photos taken by your own camera (below); a photo received in a chat, by AirDrop or through a shared album never proposes anyone;
    • invitees of the calendar entries held at a stay: invited to 'Lunch';
    • the voices and participants of a recording: spoke in 2 calls; of a meeting note-taker's transcript (Granola, Meet): in the meeting notes of 'Boat plans'.

    A day you already answered is not asked again (a with note, or a note at the stay that says with them); nor a day you said not-with; nor a home stay of someone who lives with you. The strongest come first (the most days, then the most evidence), ten at most, the rest counted. A name the record does not place is counted for logbook people review, never listed. No number stands beside a name: the order is the only rank, and the counts live in the sentence of the evidence.

    Each person's number is their place in the queue, from 1, as the rest of review numbers its proposals; it is printed on a line of its own under them, --accept 1, never beside the name, and in --json it is the person's place in people. The answers are the acts above:

    $ logbook review with --week 2026-W24 --accept 1                     # every day of it that has begun
$ logbook review with --week 2026-W24 --accept 1,2                   # two of them
$ logbook review with --week 2026-W24 --accept 1 --days 2026-06-10   # some days
$ logbook review with --week 2026-W24 --not Ola                      # not with me

    The numbers are the queue's as it stands when you answer: once someone is answered they leave it, and the people below them move up, so list the queue again before the next --accept. A name given to --accept is refused in one sentence that names the number form; --not still takes a name, and so does logbook with --from D1 --to D2 NAME, for someone the queue does not ask about. Any option review with does not take (another kind's, such as --top or --since) is refused in one sentence, and nothing is written.

    Your people in a city

    $ logbook people city Bergen --trip 2026-07-02
your people in Bergen, on journey trip:2026-07-01:2026-07-03
  Per Hansen  not yet

    The people the record places in a city, by their lives-in note, and with --trip (the journey, trip:FIRST:LAST or a day of it) whether each was seen on it: confirmed by the with module at one of its stays (a timed calendar entry they accepted, their voice or name in a recording, a note there that says with them), or stated with you on one of its days (logbook with, or an accepted answer of the queue). Names and seen / not yet, never a count. No contacts import keeps a city today (the iOS and Google contacts and WhatsApp's keep names, numbers and addresses only), so the lives-in note is the only source. --journey is --trip's old name here, kept until the release after 1.0 and said once on stderr; everywhere else --journey is people nearby's, a journey or a flight line.

    Your own camera

    A photo proposes a face for presence only when your own camera took it. What each library keeps of that today:

    Library What it keeps What is missing
    Apple Photos provenance: camera (a live photo, a camera video, an import by the back or front camera, ZIMPORTEDBY 1 or 2, or by the Camera app), received (another app, AirDrop or sharing, shared with you: 3, 8, 13), screenshot, other; and extra.imported_by which device of yours took it, and who added a photo to an iCloud Shared Library
    Immich provenance from the EXIF (camera: a make and model and an original capture time, or a live pair; received: a messenger's file name and no EXIF), and camera, the make and model which of the server's users and devices uploaded it (Immich's ownerId, deviceId): a partner's phone uploading to the same library reads as camera too
    Google Photos (Takeout) the origin: mobileUpload is camera, a shared album received the device

    So the rule (who_was_there.own_camera) is: provenance is camera, and no import the line keeps says it was received. Telling your phone from a partner's in one Immich library needs the adapter to keep the uploader, which is a change to the adapter, for later.

    Meeting note-takers

    The record reads Granola and Google Meet today. Granola's notes become a transcript/v1 line, tier 3, whose participants are the meeting's attendees as Granola names them (name and email), and its summary a separate note/v1 line, tier 2. Meet writes one event/v1 line per conference, tier 1, its signed-in participants as attendees, and its transcripts as transcript/v1 lines, tier 3. The attendees are kept, so the queue reads them as proposals, in the meeting notes of 'Boat plans', as the with module reads them at the stay. A tier-3 line stays home under the crossing as every transcript does.

    What is not here yet

    The Day page and the timeline still show faces from any library and do not set aside not-with or lives-with: that is a change to the with module (logbook/core/present.py) the pages share, left to the slice that builds the page. The journey page's seen / not yet list and the morning reconnect read people_in_city, and are built with their pages.
