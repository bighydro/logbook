    Your own agents: Telegram bots, Claude Code and the Claude apps

    You talk to agents of your own: bots you run on Telegram, a coding assistant in Claude Code, Claude in its apps. Those conversations are part of the record. Your words in them are yours, at their tier, and read like anything else you wrote. The agent's words sit beside them, marked as machine text, and no reader takes them for a person's: an agent is never in people, never someone's company, never proposed in people review, and its "I'll have it ready by Friday" is never a promise.

    logbook add telegram-export ~/Downloads/"Telegram Desktop"/DataExport_2026-03-02   # your bot chats, both sides
logbook sync telegram                                                            # what you sent your bots since
logbook add claude-sessions ~/.claude/projects                                   # every Claude Code session on this machine
logbook add claude-sessions ~/.claude/projects --dry-run                         # what would be added; nothing written
logbook add claude-export ~/Downloads/claude-data.zip                             # your conversations in the Claude apps
logbook add claude-export ~/Downloads/claude-data.zip --dry-run                   # how many new, grown and unchanged

    The machine mark

    No payload profile has a slot for "an agent wrote this", and none is invented for it. The mark rides in the slots the two frozen profiles already leave for a source's own fields (logbook/core/machine.py reads it, and every reader below goes through it):

    Where The mark What it means
    message/v1 (RFC 0008, whose extra keeps anything else a source reports) extra.machine: true the agent wrote this line
    message/v1 extra.counterpart: {"kind": "agent", "ref": {kind, value}, "name"?, "username"?} the line belongs to a conversation with an agent, whoever wrote it; on every line of it, yours and the agent's
    transcript/v1 (RFC 0004, whose consumers ignore a participant's fields they do not know) a participant {"name": "assistant", "machine": true} that participant is an agent, and every turn under its name is machine text

    The counterpart is an observed entity of kind agent: what a source saw, keyed by its ref (provider_id telegram:<bot id> for a bot, provider_id claude-code for the assistant in Claude Code, provider_id claude for Claude in its apps), never resolved and never a person. RFC 0037 (draft) names the observed entities a review will one day place: person, org, place, asset, idea. agent is not among them yet; it is defined here, by these lines, so that an Agents page, when one exists, reads the same field. Only the literal true marks anything, and a line without the mark is read as it always was.

    A tier is where a line may travel, never who wrote it: an agent's message is tier 2 because message/v1 is, exactly as your own; a session or a conversation is tier 2 because transcript/v1 is.

    Telegram: the export

    Telegram Desktop → Settings → Advanced → Export Telegram data, Machine-readable JSON. Tick Bot chats; nothing else is needed, and the file stays small. A chat's own export (⋮ → Export chat history, in a bot's chat) has the same shape and is read the same way. The export is a folder holding result.json and the media it downloaded beside it.

    logbook add telegram-export <folder> (or the folder, or its result.json: they are sniffed) reads every chat of type bot_chat and nothing else. A personal chat, a group, a channel and your saved messages are counted (chats that are not with a bot, never read) and never opened, so no person's words come in through here. Your Telegram chats with people are not read by this adapter.

    Field From
    at the message's date_unixtime; an export older than that field has only a local time without an offset, read in the record's zone
    chat {id: <the bot's user id>, type: direct, name: <the bot's name>}
    from_me false for a message from the bot (from_id user<bot id>), true for every other: a private chat with a bot has two sides. When the export names its owner (personal_information.user_id), a message from anyone else is counted and skipped
    sender for the bot's lines: {kind: provider_id, value: telegram:<bot id>, name: <bot name>}
    text the message's text (or caption) with its formatting dropped
    media_kind the export's own classification: image, voice, video, gif, audio, sticker, document, location, contact
    reply_to the key of the message replied to, when the export holds it
    edited when the export says the message was edited; extra.edited_at is when
    extra counterpart (the bot, kind agent), machine (the bot's lines), view export, message_id (the export's own id), reply_to_message_id, forwarded and forwarded_from, media and media_missing

    Media is referenced, never copied. A file the export holds is named under extra.media by its path in the export (local_path, POSIX, resolved by its parts and never outside the folder), its SHA-256, its size, its media type and file name when the export gives them. A file the export left out ((File not included…)), or a path that would leave the folder, is extra.media_missing, and the line is written all the same. payload.media is never set: the bytes are not in the record.

    A forward is not your words. A message you forwarded to a bot keeps extra.forwarded and the name it came from; promises never reads it, since what someone else wrote is not a promise of yours.

    Telegram: live, sync telegram

    export LOGBOOK_TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKENS="<bot id>:<secret>,<bot id>:<secret>"   # BotFather's tokens, your own bots
export LOGBOOK_TELEGRAM_OWNER_ID="<your numeric Telegram user id>"
logbook sync telegram

    A token can read and send as its bot, so it is a password: it lives in the environment (or ~/.config/logbook/sync.env for the schedule), never in the record, never in a line, never printed; an error names the bot by its id or username, never by its token. The owner id is what makes "a bot's updates are only its chat with you" a rule rather than a hope: a message from anyone else is counted (from someone other than the owner, never written as theirs) and never written, so a stranger who finds a bot never speaks in your name. A message outside a private chat is counted too.

    sync telegram asks each bot three read-only methods, with GET and nothing else: getMe (who the bot is), getWebhookInfo (whether its updates go to a webhook, and how many are pending) and, only when updates are pending and no webhook is set, getUpdates?timeout=0&limit=100. It never passes offset, so it never confirms an update and your bot's own program still receives every one; it never passes allowed_updates, which would change what the bot is sent; it never calls deleteWebhook, getFile or a method that sends. A test holds it to that.

    What that leaves is narrow, and it is worth saying plainly:

    • Only your side. Telegram never sends a bot its own messages, so the bot's replies never come through sync; they come through the export.
    • Only what is still pending. The Bot API keeps an update until the bot confirms it, and for 24 hours at most. A bot that is running confirms its updates as they arrive, so sync finds little or nothing; it is for what arrived while the bot was down, and for the edits and replies around it.
    • At most the first 100 pending, since reading further would need an offset, which confirms.
    • A bot on a webhook is not read: its updates go to the webhook, and getUpdates would be refused. It is one line naming the bot, the other bots are read, and sync exits 1; sync never touches the webhook.
    • A bot that long-polls while sync asks may see one 409 Conflict and retry, as the libraries do; a bot with nothing pending is never asked, so a quiet bot is never interrupted.

    The complete conversation, both sides, is the export's. sync telegram writes the same lines by the same mapping (extra.view is bot, extra.message_id the bot's own id for the message, the counterpart with its username from getMe) and the same key, so a message the export already wrote is not written again, and the other way round. There is no watermark: what the API holds is small, every run reads it whole, and the record's dedupe is the mark. --since still leaves out what came before it.

    Why the key is not the message id

    Telegram numbers the messages of a private chat per account: the message you send your bot has one id in your account (the export's) and another in the bot's (the API's). A key on (chat, message id) would dedupe each view against itself and never one against the other. So raw_id is what both sides see alike:

    <bot id>/<unix seconds>/<me|bot>/<digest>        e.g. 900000001/1772360100/me/3f1c2a9b0d4e

    the digest being the first twelve hex characters of the SHA-256 of the text (or caption, whitespace collapsed, NFC) and of whether the message carries media. The n-th message of one chat with the same four parts gets ~n after them (three photos sent as an album, in one second, with no caption). The view's own id stays under extra.message_id.

    Two consequences, by design. An edited message is keyed by the text it has now, which is what both views give once the edit is made; when sync saw the message before the edit too, the version first sent is a line of its own, beside the edited one. And the ~n of a burst is counted within what one view saw: when sync sees only the second photo of an album whose first it never saw, the two views number it differently and the record holds that photo twice. Both are rare, both leave a line too many rather than one too few, and a duplicate can be retracted.

    Claude Code: the sessions

    Claude Code keeps every session on the machine it ran on, under ~/.claude/projects/: one folder per project, named from its working directory (/Users/ines/code/solvind-log is -Users-ines-code-solvind-log), one <session id>.jsonl per session. logbook add claude-sessions <folder> reads the projects folder, one project's folder or one session's file, each sniffed, so a bare logbook add ~/.claude/projects finds them too.

    Your turns are the text of the user entries. Never yours, and never kept as anyone's: a tool's result (the tool_result blocks, the entry's toolUseResult), which is the machine's output; an entry marked isMeta (a caveat Claude Code adds) or isCompactSummary (the model's own summary of the conversation, written in the user's role); a slash command and its output; the interrupt marker. The assistant's turns are the text blocks of the assistant entries; its thinking is never kept, and an API error is not a turn. A subagent's conversation (entries marked isSidechain, and a file named agent-*.jsonl), whose "user" is the main agent, is counted and skipped. An image you pasted is counted and never stored.

    Field From
    kind, source, tier transcript, claude-code, 2 (RFC 0004's; --tier sets another for a run)
    at, end the session's first and last entry
    participants [{"name": "owner"}, {"name": "assistant", "machine": true}]: owner is a word every reader takes for you, as me
    content the turns as JSON, {"turns": [{"speaker", "text"}, …]}, stored once in the attachment store (SPEC §1.1); its sha256 is the session's digest
    title Claude Code's own title for the session (its summary entry), else the first line of your first turn
    raw_id claude-code:<session id>
    extra session and project (the working directory's last part) and cwd, git_branch, version (Claude Code's), models, tool_calls and tools (the calls by tool name: a count, never what they did or returned), images, words ({owner, machine}), counterpart ({kind: agent, ref: {provider_id, claude-code}, name: Claude Code}), and the counts every transcript has

    A session that grew. A session goes on after an import, or is resumed. Run again, a session whose text differs from the line standing for it is written as a new line, raw_id suffixed :v2 (:v3 …), that supersedes the line before (grown since the last import); an unchanged session is the same line and nothing is written. The proposals promises read in the old line are read again in the new one under new ids, so a promise you closed against the old line shows as open again; close it once more. A session Claude Code copied into a new file when you resumed it is a second session here, as it is there.

    Claude: the export of the apps

    Your conversations with Claude in its apps (the web, the desktop, the phone) live with your account, not on a machine. Ask for them: claude.ai → Settings → Privacy → Export data. A link to a zip arrives by email; the zip holds conversations.json (every conversation, its messages in it), projects.json (your projects, when you have any) and users.json (your account). logbook add claude-export <zip> reads the zip as it came, nothing unpacked to disk; the folder it unpacks to and its conversations.json are read too, and all three are sniffed, so a bare logbook add finds them. Another app's conversations.json (one without chat_messages) is not taken for it.

    Your turns are the text blocks of the messages you sent (sender human), the assistant's the text blocks of its own; an export older than the blocks gives only text, read without the placeholder the export writes for a block it cannot show ("This block is not supported on your current device yet."). Never kept, as anyone's words: its thinking; a tool call's input (an artifact's content, a search query), counted by the tool's name; a tool's result; the thinking and the artifacts an export from before artifacts were tool calls writes inline in the text (<antThinking>, <antArtifact>: dropped, each artifact counted as an artifacts call); the text the apps extracted from a file you gave them; the conversation's own summary; a message from neither side, counted. A file you gave or uploaded is named and counted, never copied: its name, its type (the export's, else the name's suffix) and its size, never its text, preview or link; a name is cut at 255 characters. Of projects.json only a project's name is read (never its description, its instructions or its documents), and users.json is never opened.

    When the export links each message to the one before it (parent_message_uuid) and names where the conversation stands (current_leaf_message_uuid), only that branch is read: a prompt you edited or an answer you asked for again leaves the other branch behind, counted (messages on a branch the conversation left). Without the links, or when they lead to a message the export does not hold, every message, in the export's order.

    Field From
    kind, source, tier transcript, claude, 2, exactly as a Claude Code session (--tier sets another for a run)
    at, end the conversation's first and last message (its own created_at and updated_at when the messages have no time)
    participants [{"name": "owner"}, {"name": "assistant", "machine": true}], as a session's
    content the turns as JSON, {"turns": [{"speaker", "text"}, …]}, stored once in the attachment store (SPEC §1.1); its sha256 is the conversation's digest
    title the conversation's name, else the first line of your first turn
    raw_id claude:<conversation uuid>
    extra conversation (its uuid), project (the name, when the conversation names a project projects.json lists), created_at and updated_at (the conversation's), tool_calls and tools (the calls by tool name: a count, never what they did or returned), attachments (each file's name, type and size in bytes, as the export gives them), words ({owner, machine}), counterpart ({kind: agent, ref: {provider_id, claude}, name: Claude}), and the counts every transcript has

    A conversation that is not an object with a uuid and a list of messages, or that has no time at all, is skipped and counted (conversations in a shape this version cannot read); one with no words in it is counted too. An export that does not parse, or a zip that is damaged, is one sentence, and nothing is written. conversations.json is read whole, so an export takes a few times its size in memory while it is read.

    A conversation that grew (or changed: a prompt edited, an answer asked for again) is a new line, raw_id suffixed :v2 (:v3 …), that supersedes the line before, exactly as a session that grew; a conversation unchanged since the last import writes nothing, and nor does one whose text any of its lines already holds. Neither does an export older than one already imported, so a folder of exports can be added again and again: a conversation earlier than the line standing for it (by its updated_at, else by where it ends) is counted (older than the line in the record) and never written back over the newer one, its text never stored. A conversation you moved back to an earlier branch ends earlier but is later, and is a new version. Every run, and --dry-run before it, says how many conversations the export holds and how many are new to the record, grown, unchanged and older. A conversation you deleted in the apps is not in the next export, and its line stays in the record: retract it if you want it gone. A conversation you only renamed is unchanged (the text is what counts), so its line keeps the old title.

    What the readers do with them

    • people, person, rollup people, drift and the company of a stay count no line of a conversation with an agent, whoever wrote it, and no participant marked machine, whatever its name: a bot you named after your sister is still not your sister.
    • people review never observes an agent and so never proposes one, not even by its name; a record that held agent lines before this version rebuilds its observed table once.
    • promises reads only your own words in a conversation with an agent: your messages to a bot and your turns in a session or a conversation in the apps. A message a bot wrote and a message you forwarded are counted under skipped (messages_by_an_agent, forwarded_messages); an agent's turn is dropped before your turns are read, and a line inside your own turn that its text gives the agent, by its label or by its own name (assistant: …, Claude: …, Claude Code: …, pasted back), is dropped too, with the lines after it in that turn up to the next speaker's label.
    • The dream gives the model your messages with an agent marked (agent on the counterpart, machine on what it wrote) and tells it that agents are not people (dream-day/3, rule 5); an agent's words do not decide the language of your day.
    • day, show and search list and find these lines like any other: an agent's message is a message of the day, and a session a transcript of it.
