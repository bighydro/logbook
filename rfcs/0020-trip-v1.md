# RFC 0020 — payload profile `trip/v1`

Status: draft · 2026-10-01 · comment period: two weeks · amended 2026-10-02 (mode `passage`, `subject`, `evidence`, `name`, `geometry`; ADR 0018)

One movement, or one metered stop, of the owner by a paid mobility service: a ride (a taxi, an Uber), a hire car, a bus or train journey bought as a ticket, a parking session. The service keeps a record of it with a provider, a mode, a place or two, a span, and sometimes a distance and a price; this profile keeps that record as the service gave it. It is the *bought* layer of a day's movement, beside the location points that show where the owner actually went and the `flight/v1` lines for the air legs. Since the amendment it also holds one *declared* movement of an asset the owner keeps track of (ADR 0018): a sea leg from a vessel's deck log, kept by hand, with the vessel as `subject`.

## Line

`kind` MUST be `trip`. `tier` SHOULD be 1 (SPEC §4: the owner's own movement, like a location point or a calendar entry); a producer that carries `price` SHOULD set 3 (money), and `logbook add --tier` overrides either. `at` is the start; `end` the end, or null when the source does not know it. `tz` is the record's zone unless the source names one. `source` is the adapter: `easypark`, `uber`, ….

## Payload

| Field | Type | Req | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `schema` | `"trip/v1"` | MUST | |
| `raw_id` | string | MUST | the source's own stable id for this record, suffixed with the source's time for it when the same id can be reported again (rule 4). The dedupe key |
| `mode` | string | MUST | `ride`, `parking`, `rental`, `transit` or `passage` (a leg under way of a vessel, rule 6) |
| `provider` | string | MUST | the service, as the adapter spells it: `easypark`, `uber`, … |
| `from` | object | MUST | the place the trip started — for a parking session, where the car stood: `{ name?, address?, code?, operator?, latitude?, longitude? }`, the source's own words and coordinates, never resolved (rule 2); at least one key present |
| `to` | object | MAY | the same shape, where it ended; absent for a parking session |
| `distance_m` | integer | MAY | metres, as the source measured it |
| `price` | object | MAY | `{ amount, currency }`, a decimal as a string and an ISO 4217 code |
| `status` | string | MAY | `completed` or `cancelled`; absent means completed |
| `supersedes` | string | MAY | id of the earlier line for the same record |
| `subject` | string | MAY | whose movement this is: an asset id as `assets.json` spells it (RFC 0001 rule 2, ADR 0018). Absent means the owner, as on a location line |
| `evidence` | string | MAY | `declared` when the record is the owner's or the keeper's word (a deck log), as `flight/v1` names it; absent means the service's own record |
| `name` | string | MAY | the movement as a person says it, `Cannes → Genoa Molo Vecchio`; a reader shows it in place of the two places |
| `geometry` | object | MAY | the planned or recorded path as a GeoJSON LineString, `{ type, coordinates: [[lon, lat], …] }`, when the source has one (an ECDIS route) |

Anything else the source reports MAY be kept under `extra`: the parking area's type, the ride's product, the vehicle's type. A payment card, a licence plate and the driver's name are not observations of the owner's day and MUST NOT be kept.

## Rules

1. **One line per record the service stores.** A ride that was requested and cancelled is a line with `status: "cancelled"` (RFC 0009 rule 3: the plan existed). Splitting a ride into legs, or joining a parking session to the drive before it, is engine work.
2. **Places are the source's.** `from` and `to` carry the service's name, address, code and coordinates as given; turning them into the record's places is a `resolution/v1` line or an engine's pass, never the adapter's.
3. **A parking session is a trip with one place.** `mode` is `parking`, `from` is the area, `at` the start and `end` the end when the source has it. The owner did not move; the car's stay is what was bought, and a reader that lists the day's movement shows a stop here.
4. **A last-used record is a line with no end.** Some services keep no sessions, only the places last used and when (EasyPark's recent parkings). Such a record is one line per place: `at` the service's time, `end` null, and `extra.observed` = `"last_used"` so a reader knows the span is not known, not zero. `raw_id` carries that time, so the next use of the same area is a new line.
5. **A price does not make a transaction.** `price` is what the service said the trip cost; the payment is a `transaction/v1` line (to be written) when a bank adapter reports it, and the two are joined downstream.
6. **A passage is a declared leg of an asset.** `mode` `passage` carries `subject` (the vessel), `evidence` `declared`, `from` and `to` in the log's words with a position when one was found (a route's end, a named place, a port table; `extra.positions` says which), `at` the departure and `end` the arrival, null while the leg is open (`extra.open`). The vessel's position at each positioned end with a time is a `location/v1` line of the same `subject` beside it, so the readers that derive an asset's track see the leg (ADR 0018); the trip line is the leg as the keeper wrote it. Whether the owner was aboard is still derived from the owner's own track, never written here (ADR 0018 rule 3).

## Producers

| `source` | `mode` | What it reads |
|---|---|---|
| `easypark` | `parking` | the EasyPark app's `recentparkings_<user>.json` from an iPhone backup (`logbook import-backup`, source `easypark`): one record per recently used area — id, signage code, area number, operator, description, type, coordinates, `lastModified` in seconds since 2001-01-01 UTC. The app keeps no sessions on the phone, so each record is a rule 4 line |
| `passages` | `passage` | a deck log transcribed as CSV (`dep_local, dep_place, arr_local, arr_place, tz, note, check`), `logbook add passages <csv> --asset <id> [--routes <dir>]`: one line per row, provider `deck-log`, `raw_id` `passages:<asset>:<dep_local>:<dep_place>`, positions from the RTZ route whose name names both places (its waypoints as `geometry`, its length as `distance_m`), else `places.json`, else the bundled port gazetteer; a row the transcriber doubts carries `extra.check`. See `docs/adapters/passages.md` |
| `uber` | `ride` | no producer yet. The Uber app's container in an iOS backup holds saved places and the app's own analytics, not the ride history, which lives on Uber's servers; the input for this producer is Uber's data export, and the mapping is `mode: ride`, `from`/`to` the pickup and drop-off, `distance_m`, `price`, the product under `extra` |

```json
{"at":"2026-09-15T14:05:08Z","end":null,"tz":"Europe/Oslo","source":"easypark","kind":"trip","tier":1,
 "payload":{"schema":"trip/v1","raw_id":"easypark:114681@2026-09-15T14:05:08Z","mode":"parking","provider":"easypark",
 "from":{"name":"Storgata 1-36","code":"8291","operator":"Oslo kommune","latitude":59.9139,"longitude":10.7522},
 "extra":{"area_type":"OnStreet","observed":"last_used"}}}
```

## Notes

- **Why one profile for a ride and a parking.** Both are a mobility service's record of the owner's car or seat: a provider, a place, a span, a price. Two profiles would differ in one field (`to`). The `mode` keeps them apart for a reader that wants only rides.
- **Why not `location/v1`.** A parking area's coordinates say where the car stood, not where the owner was; and a ride's two places are a span, not a point. The owner's own positions stay in the location lines; an engine can confirm one with the other.
- **A ticket is a trip observed through its validity.** A transit app keeps the ticket it sold (the stations, the day, the price) and, separately, the journey the owner looked up and took (clocks, legs). `sbb` writes both as `transit` lines: the ticket's `at`/`end` are its validity window, marked `extra.observed: "ticket"`, the journey's are its clocks, marked `extra.observed: "journey"`, with the legs under `extra.legs`; joining the two is a reader's or an engine's step, as with a ride and its payment (rule 5).
- **What a trip is not.** Not a flight (`flight/v1` has its own key and merge), not a calendar entry (a plan), not a stay (an engine's derivation).
- **Why a passage is a trip and not only two location lines.** The two positions say where the vessel was at two instants; the log's row says that these two were one leg, in the keeper's words, with its remark and its planned route. The location lines feed the track; the trip line keeps the leg. The amendment of 2026-10-02 is backward compatible: a line written before it has no `subject` and means what it always meant.
