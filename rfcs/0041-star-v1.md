# RFC 0041 — payload profile `star/v1`: a line worth keeping

Status: draft · 2026-10-14 · comment period: two weeks

The owner's mark on one line of the record: *this one*. A dinner in the calendar, a sentence in a note, a call, a mail thread, a flight: any line the owner wants to find again among the thousands a day can hold. The mark is the owner's act, made at the command line (`logbook star`) or on the Day page of `logbook serve`, and it is recorded as such: never inferred, never proposed by a reader, never written by a model.

`star/v1` is a new profile, not one of the RFC 0031 freezes: its schema may change while this RFC is a draft. It exists because the mark the record already had, `keeper/v1` (RFC 0024), is frozen with `photo` as a MUST field and marks photos only. RFC 0036 (open question 3) reads *a star* as a keeper line; this RFC keeps that reading for photos and gives every other line a mark of its own.

## Line

`kind` MUST be `star`. `source` MUST be `manual`: the owner made the mark, whether at the command line or through the page, which calls the same function. `at` MUST be the starred line's `at`, so a star is on its line's day and a reader of a day finds it among that day's lines (SPEC §3.2.1); `end` is null.

`tier` MUST be the starred line's tier. A tier is where a line may travel (SPEC §4), and a star travels with its line: a page shared at tier 1 carries the stars on its tier-1 lines and nothing about the lines it leaves home; a star on a tier-3 transaction is tier 3. The tier never says who made the mark. A star's `note` keeps the same tier, because the note is a caption that travels with the star; the owner's words for home belong in a `note/v1` line (RFC 0010).

## Payload

| Field | Type | Req | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `schema` | `"star/v1"` | MUST | |
| `line` | string | MUST | the `id` of the starred line |
| `kind` | string | MUST | the starred line's `kind`, so a reader can say what was starred without opening it |
| `note` | string | MAY | the owner's words on the star, one line (`logbook star --note`) |
| `supersedes` | string | MAY | the `id` of the earlier star on the same line this one replaces (a new note) |

Anything else MAY be kept under `extra`. A reader ignores what it does not know.

## Rules

1. **The owner's act.** A star is written only for a mark the owner made: `logbook star LINE`, or the star on a row of the Day page, which calls the same function. No reader, adapter or model writes one; a reader that wants to suggest a star shows its suggestion and writes nothing (ARCHITECTURE: agents never write a confirmation).
2. **A photo is starred with `keeper/v1`.** A star on a `photo/v1` line is a `keeper/v1` line of lane `memory` (RFC 0024), `source` `manual`, at the photo's tier, its `note` under the field RFC 0024's amendment of 2026-10-14 adds: the Day's hero photo and the press's pick (`year --print`, `trip --print`). A writer never writes `star/v1` for a photo line, and a photo with a `memory` keeper standing, the library's favourite included, is starred already.
3. **One star per line.** A line has at most one star standing: the latest `star` line naming it that is not retracted and not named in a later star's `supersedes` (SPEC §3.2, *standing*). Starring a starred line again with another note writes a new star that `supersedes` the standing one; with the same note, or none when it had none, nothing is written.
4. **Unstarring is a retraction.** `logbook unstar` writes a `retraction/v1` line (RFC 0003) whose `supersedes` is the standing star (or each standing `memory` keeper of a photo), at the star's tier, so the unstar travels where the star did, whichever of the line's marks the owner named (the line, its star, or a star it superseded). A star never says "not starred".
5. **Not a mark on a mark, nor on a draft.** A star names a line that is standing and is not itself a star, a keeper or a retraction, nor a model's draft (`draft/v1`, RFC 0036: the dream's draft of a day, a call's synthesis). A draft is a proposal, not the owner's to keep as a memory: RFC 0036 (open question 3) invents no mark on a draft, and what a draft says is confirmed by signing the day (RFC 0034) or by starring the lines it rests on; an event a draft proposed is starred once the owner has confirmed it, as the `event/v1` line it became. A star on a line retracted after it was written does not stand: a reader drops it, and a crossing never carries it without its line (`export share day`, `export trip-bundle` and `export crossing` alike), nor a photo's `memory` keeper without its photo, the library's favourite included.
6. **A star shows nothing its line does not.** A reader shows a star only beside a line it shows itself, through its own gate: a star never makes visible a line above a reader's tiers, nor a draft the `dream` entry of the crossing policy withholds (RFC 0036 amendment 1); a star on a draft, should one be in the record, is not shown.
7. **A star derives nothing.** It is not evidence of a stay (SPEC §3.2.3 rule 5), not company (§3.2.5), not a promise. A reader of a day marks the starred lines and may list them; nothing else changes.

## What a Day shows

The Day (`logbook day`, the Day page of `serve`) marks each starred line where it is listed with ★: an attached event, transcript, note, mail thread or call, an unplaced item, an all-day entry, a flight, and the owner's goal, review and chapter lines of the day (RFC 0036). The Day page carries the star on every one of those rows, and on a proposed event the owner confirmed (the event line it became); never on the draft's sentences, a proposal not confirmed, a call's synthesis or the row of a draft the policy withholds. The JSON gains `stars`, the day's `star/v1` lines standing, each `{line, star, kind, note}` (`star` the id of the star line), only on a day that has one; SPEC §3.2.7's object is otherwise unchanged, and a photo's star is its keeper, listed under the row's `keepers` and as the day's hero exactly as before (the keeper fixture of `conformance/profiles/` is unchanged). The Day page also marks a starred photo in its list of the row's photos. The Days page of `serve` lists the stars of its window, photos' among them, under *Starred*.

## Example (synthetic)

Ines Nordmann, Oslo, stars Tuesday's dinner in Zürich, a timed calendar entry at tier 1:

```json
{"at":"2026-06-16T17:00:00Z","end":null,"tz":"Europe/Oslo","source":"manual","kind":"star","tier":1,
 "payload":{"schema":"star/v1","line":"019cadd3-6bc0-7dcd-9133-000000000401","kind":"event",
 "note":"the table by the river"}}
```

and takes it back a week later:

```json
{"at":"2026-06-23T19:12:00Z","end":null,"tz":"Europe/Oslo","source":"manual","kind":"retraction","tier":1,
 "payload":{"schema":"retraction/v1","supersedes":"019cadd3-6bc0-7dcd-9133-000000000402","seq":5212,"reason":"unstarred"}}
```

## Notes

- **Why not `keeper/v1` with a `line` field.** RFC 0031 rule 1 lets a frozen profile gain a MAY field and nothing else: `photo` stays a MUST, and a keeper that names an event would be printed by the press as a frame with no picture. A second profile costs one reader branch; bending the first would cost every reader of photos.
- **Why the starred line's tier.** A star is metadata about its line and goes where the line goes. Tier 2 because the owner made it would keep the stars of a tier-1 day out of the page the owner shares; tier 1 always would put the time of a tier-3 payment on that page.
- **Why `at` is the line's.** So that a star is on its line's day for every reader that lists a day, for the share of that day, and for a trip bundle, with no join across days. When the mark was made is the line's `recorded_at`.
- **Why no `raw_id`.** No source has a star to import; a star unstarred and starred again is a new act and a new line.
