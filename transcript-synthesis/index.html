    The synthesis of a call

    A model reads one transcript, a work call, and writes one line about it: what someone is to do after the call, on top; the topics, each theme once however often the call came back to it; what was decided; what was left open; and a short personal part. Every item points at the utterances it rests on. The line is a draft/v1 (RFC 0036, the profile) of unit event whose subject is the transcript line, and the Day shows it under the call's row, collapsed.

    logbook transcript synthesize 01a1377b-5609-7db6-955c-a2a2b3db9660   # one call, by its line id
logbook transcript synthesize '#5432'                                 # by its seq
logbook transcript synthesize a2b3db9660                              # by the end of its id, as the Day shortens it
logbook transcript synthesize --day 2026-06-10                        # every call of a day
logbook transcript synthesize --day yesterday --dry-run               # ask the model, check the answer, write nothing

    --show-prompt prints the prompt and the material as they are sent; --again asks once more when this model has already synthesized the call. The morning's dream --catch-up synthesizes every call of the last 7 days that has no synthesis yet, or whose synthesis was made under another version of the dream entry, and never an older one unless you ask with --since (The week the close covers).

    What a synthesis is, and is not

    A synthesis is a proposal; the transcript is the record. Nothing derives a fact from it: not a task, not a promise, not a person. An action it proposes is not a task until you make it one; a decision it reports is the model's reading of the call. The synthesis never writes a note, a task or a signature. It writes one draft line, source model:<name>, and nothing else; a synthesis again writes a new line whose supersedes names the one before, and the earlier text stays in the record.

    The policy entry and the endpoint

    It runs on the dream's engine, so it is governed by the same dream entry of policy/crossing.json and reaches the same model (the dream, the endpoint): no entry, or an expired one, and it refuses in one sentence and nothing leaves the machine.

    The entry's max_tier is the ceiling, and a transcript is one line with one tier: a call above the ceiling stays home whole. The granola, transcript and wispr-flow adapters write transcript/v1 at tier 3, so under the usual "max_tier": 2 a call is held back: transcript synthesize ID says so and exits 2, --day says it in the call's row, and the catch-up counts it. To let calls cross, write "max_tier": 3 on the entry, which also lets the dream read the day's tier-3 lines (health, money), or import the calls at tier 2 (logbook add --tier 2). The draft's tier is the transcript's.

    When the entry changes

    A synthesis is a draft/v1 line derived through the dream entry, so it follows the entry, not the head, as the day's draft does (the dream; ADR 0016, Derived material follows the policy, not the head).

    • Every synthesis names the entry it was made under: extra.policy_version, the same SHA-256 of the entry's max_tier, until and model that the dream records.
    • A reader shows a synthesis only while the entry allows its tier, through the same rule as the day's draft. A synthesis above the entry's max_tier today (lowered, expired, removed) is not on the Day, not on serve's Day page and not through the MCP door. The line stays in the record. day puts one row under the call that says so and nothing of the synthesis:
          transcript   Yard plans
                   superseded by policy: tier 3, above max_tier 2 of the 'dream' entry; the call stays home until the entry allows its tier

    day --json carries the same under syntheses_withheld, one row per call (transcript, line, seq, tier, max_tier, why), a key present only then; serve shows the row as one muted line under the call; --no-dream leaves it out. A reader through a gate of its own (the MCP door) hides the synthesis without the row, counts it among the lines it withheld, and its line tool refuses it. - Synthesized again under the entry as it is now. A synthesis made under another version of the entry (an until moved, another model, a ceiling raised) is no longer unchanged: transcript synthesize sends the call again, and dream --catch-up does so for the calls of its week (or from its --since day), the row saying synthesized under another dream entry; an older call keeps its synthesis until you send it again by hand. The new synthesis supersedes the old one. A call above a lowered ceiling is above it as its synthesis is (they share the tier), so it stays home and is counted as held back; its synthesis shows again once the entry allows its tier. - A synthesis written before syntheses named their entry has no version to compare: a new entry alone synthesizes nothing again, and its tier still decides what a reader shows. doctor's drafts line names the days whose draft is withheld, not the calls.

    What the model sees

    The call and nothing else: a header, reference T, with the title, the provider, the day, the start and the end, the participants by name (never an address) and how many utterances there are; then every utterance in order, as the transcript's text in the attachment store reads (U1, U2, ...), each with its speaker as the transcript labels them and, when the recording gives times (Granola's segments, WebVTT and SRT cues), where it is in the call (20:05). A transcript whose text is not in the store, or is sealed on a machine without the identity, is said and not sent.

    The prompt (docs/synthesis-prompt.md explains it) fixes the schema and the rules: actions on top, topics consolidated across the whole call, decisions settled and not merely discussed, and work only. That last one is a rule of the prompt and not a filter in the code: the whole call reaches the model, and the model is told that anything personal goes to the personal part, at most three short neutral sentences, and nowhere else. No code decides what is personal.

    Ideas. The material also lists the labels of the ideas you have named (I1, I2, ...: the label alone), when the call is tier 2 or above, so an idea's label never raises the draft's tier; the model may propose up to three ideas the call put forward, new or a refinement of one of those, each resting on utterances of the call. They are kept beside the segments under extra.ideas (the name, the words, the idea it refines by its entity id or none, the utterances, the transcript's id), never as a segment, and wait in review idea until you say yes (ideas from calls). An idea that rests on no utterance or names a reference the material did not give is dropped. A synthesis written under the first prompt (transcript-synthesis/1) proposes none; synthesize ID sends it again under the new one.

    Evidence: the transcript line, and the utterances beside it

    A transcript's utterances are not lines of their own: transcript/v1 keeps the text in one attachment (RFC 0004), so the only line a synthesis can point at is the transcript. Every supported segment's evidence is that line's id, and which utterances it rests on is kept beside the segments, under extra.segments, one object per segment:

    {"part": "actions", "utterances": [{"n": 10, "offset": "46:30"}, {"n": 11, "offset": "47:00"}],
 "assignee": "Ola Nordmann", "due": {"phrase": "by Friday", "date": "2026-06-12"}}

    n is the utterance's place in the transcript's text, from 1, in the order transcripts.turns_of reads it; offset is where it is in the recording, when the recording says. due.date is the phrase read against the day of the call, as promises reads a due hint (SPEC §3.2.10 rule 4), and is shown with a question mark because it is a reading of the words.

    Before anything is written, every reference of the answer is checked: T, the transcript's own id, or U<n> for an utterance the material gave. An item that cites anything else, alone or beside good references (an utterance past the end, another line, a day's L1), is unsupported, as RFC 0036 rule 6 asks; an item without text is dropped; topics the model names twice are one topic, their texts joined and their utterances united; and when more than 30 % of the items are unsupported the answer is refused and nothing is written, as the dream refuses. The line holds the segments, the ids and the utterance numbers, never the prompt, the material or the call's words.

    Once, and again

    A call the same model has synthesized under the same prompt (transcript-synthesis/2, kept in extra.prompt) and the same dream entry (extra.policy_version) is unchanged: nothing is sent, nothing is written, and the standing synthesis is printed. Another model, a new prompt, another entry, or --again writes a new line that supersedes the standing one. A transcript that is retracted or superseded is not the call as the record stands and is refused.

    The run holds the record's writer lock and reads the head again before it appends: a head that moved under the reading is HeadMoved, one sentence, nothing written.

    The week the close covers

    dream --catch-up, the 06:00 close's second step (the schedule), synthesizes, after the days, each call of the last 7 days that no model has synthesized yet, or whose synthesis was made under another version of the entry, one row each; a call held back by the ceiling or without its text is counted in the closing line. A call's day is its own: the local day of its start (at) on the record's clock, day_start included, so a call at 05:30 under a day that begins at 06:00 is on the day before, and a call held three years ago and imported last night is three years old. Today's calls wait for the next morning, as today's draft does.

    An older call is never sent by the close. A record that holds years of transcripts would otherwise send every one of them to the model the first morning after an upgrade: unasked, and the whole backlog's cost in one night. It is the rule a source keeps on its first run, which never reads back further than its window without an explicit --since. The calls before the week that have no synthesis are counted and named in one line, with the two ways to them, from the oldest one's day:

    $ logbook dream --catch-up
2026-06-06  empty     nothing logged
…
2026-06-14  dreamed   #5440 · no draft yet · 4 segments
2026-06-18  dreamed   #5441 · no draft yet · 3 segments
2026-06-14  synthesized #5442 · “Hull survey” · 6 segments
2026-06-18  synthesized #5443 · “Mooring lines” · 6 segments
2 dreamed · 12 empty · 2 synthesized
1 call before 2026-06-13 not synthesized; run `logbook transcript synthesize --day 2026-05-11` or `dream --catch-up --since 2026-05-11`
    • logbook dream --catch-up --since DAY synthesizes the calls from DAY on, not only the week's, and only those: it is the explicit ask that reaches back, a day at a time or a year at once. The days it dreams are still the last --days N (14 by default); --since is about the calls. A --days under 7 narrows the calls to those days too; it never widens them.
    • logbook transcript synthesize --day DAY (or ID) is unchanged: it sends the calls you name, whatever their age.
    • Either way, a call already synthesized by the same model, under the same prompt and the same dream entry, is never sent again (Once, and again, above). A call above the ceiling is not among the ones named: it stays home whatever you ask, and only doctor counts it.

    The scheduled close runs dream --catch-up with nothing after it, so it never reaches further than the week. logbook doctor counts the calls without a synthesis on one synthesis line (the doctor): those of the week and today, which the close will synthesize; those before it, which the close leaves, with the --since that reaches the oldest; and those above the ceiling.

    On the Day

    Under the call's row, one row, collapsed: what the synthesis holds and which model wrote it. A synthesis is the call's draft, never the day's: the Day's own draft (dream day) is of unit day alone and is what it is without the synthesis, above the note as ever, and each keeps its one standing draft (RFC 0036 amendment 1).

      09:00–12:00  stay   Home · 3 h · 1 transcript
      transcript   Yard plans
                   synthesis · 1 action, 2 topics, 1 decision, 1 open thread, 1 personal · model:qwen2.5-7b-instruct

    The items are behind it: in serve, a toggle under the call opens them, actions on top, each with its assignee and due hint and where in the call it rests (the line on hover), an unsupported one muted and marked; day --json carries them under syntheses; transcript synthesize ID prints them:

    2026-06-10 10:00 “Yard plans”: synthesized as #5441 draft/v1 · model:qwen2.5-7b-instruct · tier 3 · 11 utterances read at b7e3ddf2 · 6 segments, 1 unsupported
    action    Send the survey report to Ines.  → Ola Nordmann · due by Friday (2026-06-12?)  [46:30, 47:00]
    topic     Hull survey: The yard in Vollen surveys in week 26; the keel is to be checked too.  [02:00, 20:00, 45:00]
    topic     Mooring lines: The new mooring lines arrived.  [08:30]
    decision  The survey goes to the yard in Vollen.  [12:00, 12:20]
    open      Who pays for the haul-out is still open.  [31:00]
  ~ personal  A family matter of Ines's came up.  unsupported
  a synthesis is a proposal: the transcript is the record

    A synthesis the dream entry no longer allows is not among them: its row says superseded by policy and why (When the entry changes). day --no-dream leaves syntheses, and that row, out with the day's draft. show lists the draft line on the day it was written, as every line.
