    The commands

    logbook has 32 commands, grouped here by what you are doing. logbook --help is this list on one screen; logbook <command> --help has a command's arguments. Everything that was a command of its own before 0.6 still answers to its old name: it prints one line saying the new one and runs it (the table at the end, generated from the code by scripts/command_aliases.py).

    One writer at a time: a command that appends (add, sync, import backup, and every other through append) holds state/writer.lock in the record folder for its whole run, a sync's walk included. A second writer says another logbook command is writing to this record (sync immich, since 2026-09-10T06:00:00Z); waiting and waits; with the global --no-wait it refuses and exits 2. A lock whose process is gone is taken over with one line saying so. Readers (show, day, verify, every reader) never take it. The lock is the courtesy between writers; what keeps the chain whole is that a writer reads logbook.json again before every batch and writes nothing when the head has moved.

    Start

    Command What it does
    init create a logbook (default ~/Logbook)
    setup the guided first run, one question at a time, resumable; and the record's settings: setup places, setup assets, setup questions, setup day_start (where your day begins), setup phone (whether your own phone writes through the Library behind Tailscale Serve, off by default, from your phone), setup agents list|add SENDER [--name NAME]|remove SENDER (your own bots by the sender id Beeper gives them, whose lines add beeper and sync beeper mark machine text, your own agents), setup speakers (what separates voices on this machine and the voiceprints it keeps; --fetch-model downloads its models, the one network event; --rebuild makes the voiceprint registry again from the record, speakers); setup map PATH names the offline OpenStreetMap file maps are drawn from, its header read first, --off to remove it (the map); setup places review names the places you return to from what the record already knows, offline, and with --ask-photos from what your photo libraries say about the photos already in it (places)
    doctor the check-up, one line per check, exit 1 on a fail, the crossing ceilings, the record's home (ADR 0022), the last scheduled run, the last restore test and the serve service among them, and lines dated outside their source's plausible range (--implausible lists them, The doctor); --attachments lists the lines whose attachment the store lacks, by source (the attachment store); --packet [--out FILE] is a diagnostic to send a collaborator, with no line's content, no name, no home path and no value in it, and one probe of the dream endpoint when it is set (Sending a diagnostic); doctor sources lists every adapter and, with --gaps, where each went quiet

What you heard); --install-schedule runs sync --scheduled (sync --all, then doctor, the outcome to state/last-run.json, one message when something is wrong; a source's first scheduled run reads its last 14 days, never its whole history, the schedule) at 00:00, 12:00 and 18:00, and at 06:00 sync --scheduled --close, which closes the day (the dream's catch-up after the sync); --system for a machine nobody logs in to
    goal your goals, one goal/v1 line each in your own words, tier 2 (goals): goal add TEXT --horizon life\|year\|quarter [--from DAY] [--until DAY] [--supersedes GOAL] writes one (a goal reworded is a new goal that supersedes the old); goal review GOAL advancing\|stalled\|done\|let-go\|superseded [--note TEXT] [--at DAY\|RFC3339] [--period day\|week\|month\|quarter\|year] your verdict on it, on the day it is about (written in October about August, it is on the August Day); goal list [--open] [--json] every standing goal with its latest review; goal import FILE [--horizon H] [--from DAY] [--review DISPOSITION [--at DAY] [--period P] [--note TEXT]] [--dry-run] the first backfill, one goal per line of a text file (text \| horizon \| from), one verdict for all of them when given

    status status KIND ENTITY DISPOSITION [--reason "…"] [--at YYYY-MM-DD] [--portfolio "…"]: what you decided about an org (hold, add, sell, let-go, exited, passed), an idea (spark, exploring, build, built, killed), a place (let-go) or an asset (sold, let-go), one status/v1 line, tier 3 for an org and 2 for the rest, the reason on it, and for an idea, a place or an asset also as a tier-2 note (an org's reason stays on its tier-3 line); --portfolio names which portfolio holds an org, on that line ("" clears it; an org's only); a place or asset it closes keeps its registry entry, closed with until; status list KIND (the profiles)
    star star LINE [--note TEXT] marks a line worth keeping: one star/v1 line at the line's own time and tier, so it travels where the line does; a photo's star is a memory keeper, the Day's hero and the photo the press picks; starred again with another note, the new note supersedes (the acts)
    unstar unstar LINE takes a star back: a retraction of the line's star, or of each memory keeper of a photo, at the star's tier; nothing is rewritten (the acts)
    with with DAY NAME [NAME…] [--at HH:MM] says who was there when the record could not see: one note/v1 line per person, with <name>, which people and the Day read as company; a name the record does not place is refused with the people review hint (the acts); with --from D1 --to D2 NAME the same line on every day of a stretch of 60 days at most, each at that day's longest stay in its waking hours, day_start to midnight (who was there)

    digest one day in 25 lines at most, closing with one question
    search full-text search through the index: words, "a phrase", kar*
    people everyone the record names, never the owner; people --priority ranks them by a plain score; people NAME is one person's page; people gifts [NAME] [--json] the gifts you gave and were given, in the day's order, or one person's (gifts); people merge the same person named twice; people review who the sources saw that the record has not placed, proposed against the people it names, accepted or rejected only when told, --from-google with your Google contacts as names for it, never lines; people drift [--min-days N] [--quiet-days M] [--year YYYY] [--show] who you used to see and have drifted from; people nearby PLACE \| --journey ID \| --days FROM TO [--radius KM] who has been seen where you are about to go (people); people mail [--since DAY] [--until DAY] [--json] which mail is a person's (someone the record names as a person sent it to you, or you sent it to one) and which is other mail (nobody named, a no-reply sender, an agent), counted over the window, the last 30 days by default, never an address or a name in the terminal, --json with person ids and never an address; the rule the crossing sends mail by (people's mail); people ingest NAME --window FROM TO and people ask NAME QUESTION [--show-prompt], the relationship reader, experimental: the model of LOGBOOK_DREAM_ENDPOINT answers from the person's lines, and the days you spent together (their dates and the places' names, never coordinates), at or under the relationship entry's max_tier, signed days only unless it says include_unsigned, every claim citing them or listed as unsupported, nothing written (the relationship reader); people not-with NAME --from D1 --to D2, people lives-with NAME --from D [--until D] and people lives-in NAME CITY your word on who was there, one note each, and people city CITY [--trip ID] your people in a city, seen or not yet on a journey (--journey there is its old name until the release after 1.0) (who was there)

    Derive

    Command What it does
    derive derive stays reads the record into stays and moves, appending nothing; derive flights and derive keepers infer lines; derive transcripts voice-memos and derive descriptions keepers run a local model, nothing leaving the machine; derive transcripts separates the voices too when the speakers extra and its models are here (--no-speakers turns it off), and derive speakers hears again the transcripts heard before, each into a line that supersedes the old (speakers)

    dream dream day YYYY-MM-DD [--dry-run] [--show-prompt]: a model reads the day under the dream entry of policy/crossing.json and writes one draft/v1 line, the day in a paragraph, the threads it moved, two questions, the people and promises it proposes, every sentence with the lines it came from or marked unsupported, and the events it thinks the day held, kept in the draft until you accept one; a proposal, shown on the Day above your note, never a fact; dream journey ID drafts a journey once it has come home (show trips and show trip carry it), and dream week [DAY\|YYYY-Www] an ISO week once its Sunday is over, from its seven days and their drafts, every figure the code's (the Day of its Sunday carries it; journeys and weeks); dream --catch-up [--days N] [--since DAY] dreams each of the last N days that has no draft or whose lines changed, one line per day, then each week whose seven days are among them, then synthesizes the calls of the last 7 days that have none (from DAY on with --since), naming the older ones in one line, never sending them, then each journey that came home on one of the days, then the month whose last day is among them and each chapter that closed on one (the dream); dream month [YYYY-MM] drafts a month once it is over, told from the days you signed, the people by name only and never with a count (months), and dream chapter [ID] a chapter from the month and week drafts it spans (chapters), each with --json

    videos the family's videos, which sync drive --videos names from Drive's metadata: videos fetch [--limit N] [--again] [--cache-gb N] downloads the next files nobody has inferred (with --again, the inferred ones too) into a bounded cache under state/videos/ (8 GB unless you say), one at a time, each verified against Drive's md5, a dropped connection resumed; a file bigger than the cap is refused and said so; --models fetches the speech and vision models once; the only command of the videos that uses the network (Videos)
