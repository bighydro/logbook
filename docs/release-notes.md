# Release notes

What each version changed for you, in five bullets. [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md) has every change in full; [Upgrading](tour.md#upgrading) has the two migrations a record may need.

## 0.6.0, 6 October 2026

- **Starting is a conversation, and the record reads back as days.** `logbook setup` asks one question at a time, each with its default and the reason it is asked, and `logbook doctor` says whether this machine is set up to keep the record. `logbook day` is one calendar day: the night, the stays, who was there, health, weather, what you wrote. `days` is a window of them one line each, `year` composes a year, and `digest` is the day in 25 lines ending in one question that now comes from your own record and a policy file you can edit.
- **The record comes off the screen.** `logbook serve` shows every page in a browser on this machine only. `year --html FILE --print` and `trip --html FILE --print` lay a year or a trip out for A4 or Letter with a cover and page numbers. `export vault` renders the record as a folder of Markdown with wikilinks for Obsidian or Logseq. `logbook mcp` serves it to an agent on this machine behind a tier gate that defaults to tier 1.
- **More of your life reads back.** `search` across notes, transcripts, messages, mail and events; `ledger` for your money by month or trip; `people` and `person` for everyone the record knows, with `people merge` for the duplicates and `rollup people --drifting` for the ones you are losing touch with; `promises` for the sentences in your own words that read as commitments, with a local model to judge them; `tasks` with the evidence one was done; `rollup listen` and `rollup attention` for what you listened to and where your screen time went.
- **You hand a day or a trip to a friend.** `share day` writes a signed bundle of one day for one named member of your circle, `export trip-bundle` does the same for a whole trip at the tier you choose, and `receive` verifies the signature and keeps their side beside your record, never in its chain, so a shared trip has a merged page with both sides' words and photos. `backup` copies the record to another disk, verifies it there and restores it.
- **New sources and better old ones.** Apple Wallet, Spotify, Apple Music, Apple Podcasts from a phone backup, Screen Time, weather, Apple Photos faces and albums, voice memos transcribed by a local model, ten more Google Takeout products, a 20 GB Takeout mailbox read in chunks, and a ship's deck log as declared passages. `logbook demo --years 40` writes a whole synthetic life to try it all on. Three Takeout adapters that silently skipped every row of a current export read them again, and every reader now works in a pipe and on a timezone this machine's zone database does not know.

## 0.5.0, 1 October 2026

- **An iPhone backup is a dozen sources in one go.** `logbook import-backup <folder> --only <sources>` reads WhatsApp, iMessage, calls, Photos, Health, Books, Voice Memos, Wallet, Reminders, Safari, Podcasts, Flighty, Splitwise and more from a Finder or iTunes backup, encrypted ones included with your password, locally. Nothing in the backup is modified, and a source you switch off in `policy/import.json` stays off.
- **Health is right, and a wrong record is repaired.** Resting heart rate and heart rate variability are stored as the Health app keeps them, in beats per minute and milliseconds; earlier imports were 60 and 1,000 times too large. `logbook repair health-units` writes a corrected line and a retraction for each wrong one, nothing rewritten. Withings and MyFitnessPal join Apple Health as sources, and a night's sleep is summed properly across devices.
- **Your trips, places and company are derived from the record.** `trips` finds runs of nights away and names them by place or airport; `derive stays` turns a track into stays and moves; the rollups sum countries, flights, nights, places and people over a year; and `show person`, `show place` and `show asset` are pages read from the whole record. Who was with you comes from calendars, transcripts, notes and tagged faces, and you are never counted as your own company.
- **Boats and aircraft are subjects of their own.** `assets.json` registers the vessels and aircraft whose tracks the record keeps, AIS and ADS-B feed their positions live, a flight you declare with `add flight` is evidence, and `infer flights` finds the ones your tracker shows. `verify` streams a record of any size, dedupe runs through the index, and `export crossing` hands a window of lines to one named destination at a tier ceiling.
- **The format grew without breaking.** Thirteen payload profiles were written down as RFCs (calls, flights, health samples, mail, tasks, browsing, watching, listening, trips, transactions, highlights, voice memos, keepers), the conformance sample carries one line of each, and the independent TypeScript implementation agrees with this one on a record either writes, checked on every pull request.
