# ADR 0023 — Digital events do not make a stay

Status: proposed · 2026-10-13 · narrows SPEC §3.2.3 rule 5 (SPEC 0.4); records what #263 changed in the reference

**Context.** `derive stays` clusters the owner's location points. A cluster shorter than `stay_min_s` (20 minutes) is a stop unless evidence falls inside it, and SPEC §3.2.3 rule 5 promoted it to a stay on any line of the kinds `event`, `transcript`, `note`, `call`, `message` and `photo`. The rule was written when every `event/v1` line came from a calendar (`ios-calendar`, then `ics`) or from Google Home's history of the door, and each of those puts the owner at a place at a time. Since then the record has taken events from sources that are not physical: `sync notion` writes a page edited (#253), `sync drive` a file changed (#256), and until #263 `sync gtasks` wrote a task created or completed. Such an event happens nowhere. Say Ola Nordmann changes the crew list in a Drive folder Ines shares with him at 14:05, while Ines's phone sits for ten minutes at a café in Oslo. The café became a stay with one event `attached`, and `places propose`, which reads the same stays, offered it as a place to name. The more sources a record has, the more stops become stays for reasons that say nothing about where the owner was. #263 changed the reference (`stays.somewhere`, and the same test in `reading.owner_track` for `places propose`) and left the spec as it was, so the text and the reference disagreed.

**Decision.**

1. **An event is evidence only when it happened somewhere.** It passes any of four tests: its source is a calendar (`ios-calendar`, `ics`); it is Google Home's history (source `google-takeout`, `calendar.id` `google-home`); its payload carries a `location` with something other than whitespace in it; or its payload carries `attendees`, a list with at least one member. SPEC 0.4 §3.2.3 rule 5 states the four tests exactly; in the reference they are `stays.somewhere`, `stays.CALENDARS` and `stays.HOME_HISTORY`.
2. **What the line carries decides, and a source is named only when its entries are a place and a time whatever they carry.** A Wallet pass with its venue, or a ticket with its address, promotes from any source, and a new source needs no change to the rule. An event that carries neither, from a source that is neither (Notion, Drive, a task list), is a digital event. It promotes nothing and `attached` does not count it. `show` still lists it on its day.
3. **Only events are narrowed.** #263 changed the event and nothing else, and this ADR records that and no more: transcripts, notes, calls, messages and photos promote as before, and a line of any other kind (`mail`, `task`, `browse`, …) was never evidence and is not now. Whether a message that reached the owner's phone in a pocket on a tram is presence is a separate question. A change to it is a new ADR and a new SPEC version.

**Alternatives considered.**

- *Drop events from evidence altogether.* A calendar entry and the arrival at the door are the best evidence the record has that a short stop was a visit: the ten-minute meeting, the drop-off at home. Without them, real visits would go back to being stops.
- *Name the digital sources instead (`notion`, `drive`, …).* Every new adapter that writes events would promote until someone remembered to add it to the list. Naming the physical sources and testing the payload fails closed: a new digital source promotes nothing.
- *Count a digital event when the owner made it.* For most sources the record cannot tell who changed a file. And even an edit the owner made carries no place: it could have been made from anywhere.
- *Leave the spec as it was and call the reference's test a reader's own.* §3.2 exists so that two implementations derive the same stays. §6.1 compares `segments`, and a segment's `kind`, `promoted` and `attached` all depend on this rule.

**Consequences.**

- SPEC goes to 0.4, with rule 5 and the inputs of §3.2.3 rewritten and a version history added. The format does not change: §1–§3.1 stay as they are, a record still names `logbook/0.3`, no hash changes, and no conformance fixture or head changes. Every event in `conformance/` carries attendees or a location, and every event of the seed-1 demo record of §6.1 is an `ios-calendar` entry, so each passes the tests as it passed the old rule. From 0.4 on, the document's version and the format's name are two numbers.
- A record with digital events derives some stays as stops that it used to promote. Nothing in the record changes; the next run of `derive stays`, `day`, `days`, `trips` and `places propose` prints the new reading (ADR 0013: derived is disposable).
- The `event/v1` lines `sync gtasks` wrote before #263 stay in the record. They carry no location and no attendees and come from no calendar, so they promote nothing.
- A new source whose entries are a place and a time whatever they carry joins the list in rule 5 and `stays.CALENDARS` together, and that is a SPEC version. Any other source writes `location` or `attendees` when it has them, and promotes through those.
- logbook-ts promotes on every event today (`promotes` in `src/day.ts`, at 5452b8a). Until it takes the four tests, the two implementations disagree on a record with digital events. Neither the conformance fixtures nor the demo record holds one, so no comparison that runs today shows the difference. logbook-ts is asked to confirm parity.
