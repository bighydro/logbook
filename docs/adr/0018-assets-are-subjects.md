# ADR 0018 — Assets are subjects, not places

Status: accepted · 2026-09-30

**Context.** The owner's boat, aircraft and car move, and they move without the owner. A yacht on a delivery passage, an aircraft flown by a partner, a car lent to a friend: each has a position at every instant, and the owner's phone is somewhere else. The record so far has one track, the owner's (`location/v1`, RFC 0001), and the tempting shortcut is to treat the boat as a *place* the owner is sometimes at, the way a house or an office is. That is wrong twice. A place does not move, so a visit to it can be derived from the owner's track alone; a boat's position is a second observation, made by a second instrument (an AIS or ADS-B receiver), that the owner's track cannot supply. And the interesting fact, *the owner was aboard*, is a relation between two tracks, not a property of either: the same boat position means one thing when the owner's phone is on deck and another when the owner is at home and the boat is out with the yard's skipper.

**Decision.**

1. **An asset is a subject with its own track.** A boat, an aircraft or a car is registered once in the record, in `<root>/assets.json`, as `{id, kind, name, mmsi?, icao24?, registration?}` with `kind` one of `yacht`, `aircraft`, `car`. Its positions are `location/v1` lines like the owner's, with `subject` set to the asset's id (RFC 0001, amended). The owner is never a subject: absent means the owner, and there is no id for them.
2. **A position of an asset is written as observed, by the instrument that observed it.** `logbook sync ais` asks aisstream.io for the position reports of every registered MMSI; `logbook sync adsb` asks the OpenSky Network for the state of every registered icao24. Each report is one line, `source` `ais` or `adsb`, `tracker` `aisstream` or `opensky`, `raw_id` from the instrument, the identifier and the fix time (`aisstream:<mmsi>:<unix seconds>`, `opensky:<icao24>:<unix seconds>`), so a report seen twice is one line (ADR 0017), and each asset carries its own watermark in `state/<source>.json`. What the receiver reports beyond the position (speed, course, navigational status, callsign, altitude) is kept as RFC 0001 says.
3. **"Aboard" is derived, never written.** Whether the owner was on the boat is a downstream judgement (ADR 0011, ADR 0013): an engine pass that compares the owner's track with the asset's over a window and decides, by distance and duration, that they moved together. No adapter writes it, no location line carries it, and a change of mind about the threshold changes no line in the record. The same holds for *who* was aboard: a guest's phone is not in the record, and the record does not guess.
4. **The registry is a setting, not the chain.** `assets.json` is read by the sync commands and by readers that want to name an asset; it is edited by `logbook assets add` and by hand; it is not hashed. A line whose `subject` the registry no longer names is still valid and still shown, under the id it carries. Renaming an asset is editing the registry; the lines keep the id.

**Consequences.**
- One profile, two tracks. `show` collapses runs of points by source *and* subject, so a day at sea reads as two rows (the phone's points and the boat's), and an engine that derives days, trips and places reads the owner's track by filtering `subject` absent, as before; nothing already written changes meaning.
- Every asset the owner tracks is one entry and two environment variables at most; a new instrument (a GPS tracker in the car, a satellite messenger) is a new adapter that writes the same lines with `subject` set, never a new profile.
- Identity stays with the mapping (ADR 0017 rule 3): re-running `sync ais` after a network failure, or running it from two machines, appends nothing twice.
- The record can hold an asset's history from before the owner had it or after they sold it (the receiver does not know who owns the hull). Whether such lines belong is the owner's call at sync time, by registering or removing the asset; the record records what was asked for.
