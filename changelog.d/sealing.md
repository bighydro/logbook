### Spec
- **Tiers 2 and 3 sealed at rest** (SPEC v0.3 §1, §1.1, §2, §3, §3.1, §4, §6; [RFC 0029](rfcs/0029-encryption-at-rest.md), adopted; [ADR 0020](docs/adr/0020-tiers-2-3-sealed-with-age.md)). A sealed line keeps a plain `payload` that is a reference, `{"schema": "sealed/v1", "of": "<the real schema>", "digest": "<sha256>"}`, and carries the content in `payload_enc`, outside the hash as `id` is: the RFC 8785 text of `{payload, salt}` (16 random bytes), age-sealed (X25519, binary, standard base64) to every recipient `logbook.json` names. The pre-image of §3 is unchanged, so `verify` checks the whole chain without any key, counts the lines it did not open, and, with the identity, opens every sealed line and fails on bytes that do not hash to their digest. `format` is `logbook/0.3`; a `0.2` record is read and written as it is, never migrated, and becomes 0.3 when given recipients. Attachments a tier 2–3 line names are sealed under their plaintext digest (the age header is the marker; raised, never lowered). The format's answer to RFC 0029's three options, the key handling, the migration road and what logbook-ts must implement are in the RFC; §15 says where the implementation parted from the draft. Level 2 conformance: `conformance/sample-logbook-sealed` with the published fixture identity `conformance/identity.txt`, head `c625fd32d553…` in `expected-sealed.json`, verified keyless and keyed in CI; the Level 1 sample stays a `logbook/0.2` record and its head does not change.

### Added
- `logbook key init [--recovery] [--recipient age1…]`, `key show`, `key add-recipient`, `key remove-recipient` (`logbook/sealing.py`, `logbook/store.py`, RFC 0029 §6): this machine's identity is written to `~/.config/logbook/identities/<owner_id>.txt` (`%APPDATA%\logbook\identities\` on Windows, or where `LOGBOOK_IDENTITY_FILE` or the global `--identity-file PATH` point), readable by its owner only, never in the record and never in a variable on disk; `--recovery` prints a recovery identity once, for paper, and stores it nowhere; `init` refuses to seal with fewer than two recipients; `add-recipient` and `remove-recipient` reseal every sealed line and file to the new list with no hash changed and append one `rekey/v1` line. From then on every tier 2 or 3 line appended is sealed (`Logbook.append`, `append_many`), a tier-1 line never; a writer without the identity refuses tiers 2–3 in one sentence (it cannot dedupe through the blinded index) and writes tier 1, so an unattended `sync ais` needs no key. Needs the new `sealed` extra (`pyrage`); without it the first seal or open names it. `doctor` checks it.
- `logbook seal --all` (RFC 0029 §10.2): seals the tier 2–3 lines written before the record had recipients and the files they name, the ADR 0014 way: new month files under a temporary folder, `prev` and `hash` recomputed from the first changed line, `format` 0.3, a `lineage` entry, the index dropped, one `migration/v1` line with `from_head`, `sealed_lines` and `sealed_attachments`. The plain files are replaced, not kept beside the record, and the command says once that a backup taken before still holds the plaintext.
- `logbook verify` reports sealing: `sealed: N lines, every one opened and checked against its digest`, or `not opened (no identity at …)`, and `plain at tier 2 or 3: N lines; logbook seal --all seals them`; `--keyless` never opens, so a keyed machine can show what a keyless one sees. The first line, `valid — N lines, head …`, is unchanged, so the cross-implementation job still compares it.
- `logbook export FILE` writes sealed lines opened by default (the interchange form a line re-enters by, ADR 0017) and verbatim under `--sealed`; without the identity it refuses and names `--sealed`. `show`, `day` and every reader through the index get opened lines; a reader without the identity lists the day and prints `[sealed note/v1; no identity to open it]` for such a line.
- `export crossing` (RFC 0005, new section *Sealed lines*): `policy/crossing.json` may give a destination a `recipient`; a sealed line crosses with `payload_enc` resealed to it, or under `--open` with `payload_open` = `{payload, salt}` in its place; its hashed fields are the owner's verbatim either way, and the manifest's `sealing` says which. Sealed files are opened, checked and resealed or copied opened. Without a recipient or `--open`, or without the identity, the export refuses.

### Changed
- `index.sqlite` lives in the user's cache directory, `~/.cache/logbook/<owner_id>/index.sqlite` (`~/Library/Caches/logbook/` on macOS, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\logbook\cache\` on Windows, or `LOGBOOK_CACHE_HOME`), not in the record (RFC 0029 §8): a copy of the folder, by any tool, carries no locator. A sealed line's payload columns come from its opened payload; its `raw_id` is stored as an HMAC under a key kept in the index's `meta` sealed to the recipients, so dedupe and `line_id` match either form and a chat id cannot be read or confirmed from the file; the search table holds no words of a sealed line (`logbook search` does not search sealed lines). An index built without the identity records so, and the first keyed reader rebuilds it. Schema 7. A stray `index.sqlite` left in a record by an earlier version can be deleted.
- `Logbook.attach(data, tier=None)` and `attach_file`: in a record with recipients the file is sealed unless the caller says `tier=1`; `attachment_bytes` and `attachment(sha256)` give readers the plaintext; `backup --verify` checks a sealed file when the identity is here and counts it unchecked when not.
