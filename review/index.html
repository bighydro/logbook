    Review: what the sources saw

    Your sources see more than you have named. A mail domain recurs, a group chat is about a company, a Notion page grows into a project, the receiver reports a hull the registry no longer names. logbook review is one sitting for all of it (RFC 0037): for each of the five kinds of thing the record names, what the sources observed and the record has not placed, proposed against what you named, with the evidence, numbered. Nothing is written unless you say so, and nothing is looked up: no register of companies, no code host, no Notion, no geocoder. A sixth queue is no kind of thing the record names but the same sitting: review video, the scenes of the family's videos that videos infer proposed a place or a date for (the video queue), listed after the five and before the speakers' below.

    logbook review                       # every kind with its queue, one block each; nothing written
logbook review org                   # the queue of one kind: person, org, idea, asset or place
logbook review org --accept 1 --name 3 "Fjordkiosk AS" --reject 4
logbook review idea --json           # the queue, the unplaced and the placed count, as JSON
logbook review asset --all-above 50  # accept every proposal at or above the score
logbook review video --accept 1 --date 2 "summer 1994" --ask 3   # a scene's own answers

    review person is people review and review place is setup places review: the same sitting, the same numbers, the same output byte for byte, under one name. review speakers is the voices of your recordings not yet named (RFC 0038 §4), in the same shape with answers of its own (--unnamed, --same-as, --consent, --play, --named): who they are. review goal is your goals, each asked once a week (below). review alone lists every queue, the videos', the goals' and the voices' after the five.

    The five queues

    An observed thing's identity is the strongest identifier its observation carries, never the id a source gives one observation; keyed the other way, one company explodes into a row per mail.

    Kind Observed by, strongest first Proposed against
    person an email address, a phone number, the name's words folded (people) the people the resolutions name
    org the registered domain of a mail, calendar or transcript address (a public mail provider, your own domains and a service's own hosts, a calendar's rooms among them, name nobody's org); a code-host org of a url, a task's link or an act sync github wrote (your own account is yours, never an org); a group chat's name; a ledger merchant seen on 3 lines or more the orgs: entity/v1 lines, and resolution/v1 lines of type company read as orgs
    idea a Notion page's root (a page under it is the root's idea; a database row is its own root, never the page above its database, and is proposed only when its Type says Idea, idea_rows in policy/entities.json); a code-host repository of a url, a task's own url or links, or an act sync github wrote; a task's or a transcript's title on 2 lines of 2 sources or more; never a line a bot made or an agent wrote, nor a relations line the ideas the entity/v1 lines name
    asset a track's subject the registry does not name (its MMSI or icao24 when the instrument said); an aircraft registration on 3 flights or more the assets of assets.json
    place a cluster of your stays within 75 m (places) the names the sources gave it

    The score is a plain sum of what speaks for a pair, as people review's is:

    Kind Evidence Points
    org the same domain · the same code-host org · the same name (the words in any order, AS, ASA, Ltd, Inc, GmbH, AB aside; two words, or one of six letters) · the org's people on its lines · the same days 60 · 50 · 20, +5 a further source up to +10 · 10 a person up to 30 · 2 a day up to 20
    idea the same page · the same repository · the same title (the articles aside) · its people · the same weeks 60 · 50 · 20, +5 … · 10 … · 5 a week up to 20
    asset the same identifier · the same registration spelled another way (ZZ-ABC, ZZABC) · the same name 60 · 50 · 20

    Days, weeks and people alone never propose. A domain, a code-host org, a page, a repository or a title that matches nothing, and an asset the registry does not know, is proposed as a new one, after the matches; a group chat and a merchant are only ever proposed against an org you have. Among equal scores an org's hint decides — company (a domain), community (a code-host org, a group), brand (a merchant), personal (a family's domain: every name on it is a given name and a surname, the given name the address's own, kari@ "Kari Nordmann", and they share the surname the domain spells; or a person's own code-host account, a github: handle a resolution names) — for ranking only; the hint is never written to a line.

    $ logbook review org
4 proposals · 5 observed orgs not yet placed, 1 placed · tier 2
  1  [34]  group "nordmann verft" (Nordmann Verft; message 3) → Nordmann Verft AS: same name Nordmann Verft ~ Nordmann Verft AS · 1 person on both · together on 2 days
  2  [25]  git.example.org/nordmann-verft (nordmann-verft; browse 1, task 1) → Nordmann Verft AS: same name nordmann-verft ~ Nordmann Verft AS across chrome, gtasks
  3  [new]  fjordkiosk.example.org (Fjordkiosk; mail 2) → ____
  4  [new]  nordmann.example.org (Kari Nordmann, Per Nordmann; mail 2) → ____
nothing written: `review org --accept N[,N]`, `--name N "…"`, `--reject N[,N]`, or `--all-above SCORE`

    $ logbook review asset
2 proposals · 2 observed assets not yet placed, 0 placed · tier 2
  1  [50]  ZZ-ABC (flight 3) → Lerka: same registration ZZ-ABC ~ ZZABC
  2  [new]  970123456 (GAMLE SOLVIND; location 3) → ____

    Your say

    Kind --accept / --name --reject
    person the resolution/v1 alias lines people merge writes one entity-review/v1 line, type person
    org one entity/v1 line binding the observed identifiers (a domain, a code-host org, a group, a merchant); a new org's id a UUIDv7, minted after its dedupe key is looked up, so a key that resolves already reuses its id one entity-review/v1 line, type org
    idea one entity/v1 line binding the page, the repository or the title one entity-review/v1 line, type idea
    asset a new one registered in assets.json as setup assets add writes it, under the id its lines carry; one entity/v1 line binding its identifiers one entity-review/v1 line, type asset
    place the places.json entry with its fitted radius, and the naming as one note/v1 line one entity-review/v1 line, type place, the spot's centre and the name
    video one attribution/v1 line binding the scene to the place and/or the date its draft proposes, or your own (--place, --date), beside the video's line and never inside it one scene-review/v1 line (RFC 0040 §5), never an entity-review/v1 line: no other queue reads it

    --name N "…" accepts a new one under your own name; on a proposal that matched an entity you have, it is refused (accept the match; a rename is an entity/v1 line that supersedes the standing one). A rejected pair is never proposed again; retract the line and it returns. An entity/v1 line is tier 2 and never cites a tier-3 line, so a merchant matched from the ledger is bound as a merchant ref and the payments are not its evidence; an entity-review/v1 line that cites them is tier 3. The profiles are on their own page.

    Every number is checked before the first line is written: a number the queue does not have, a number both accepted and rejected, a score that is not a whole number, or an option of another kind exits 2 and writes nothing. On a terminal, an org's, an idea's, an asset's and a place's queue asks for each proposal: y, n, a name for a new one, Enter to skip, q to stop; each answer is written at once. The video's asks the same for each scene: y, n, a to keep it to ask the family where and when it was filmed, Enter to skip, q to stop.

    The video queue

    review video is the scenes of the family's videos (Videos, RFC 0040): each scene of a file's latest inference that you have not answered, the surest proposal first, with the sentences, frames and speech it rests on. --accept takes the place or the date its draft proposes and writes one attribution/v1 line beside the video's, never inside it; --reject writes the scene's no, one scene-review/v1 line of RFC 0040's and never an entity-review/v1 line, so no other queue reads it; --json is the queue or what was written. Its own options go with it alone, and every other kind refuses them as it refuses a place's:

    Option
    --place N NAME, --date N WHEN accept scene N with your own place (a places.json name, an asset's id or name) or date (1994, 1994-07, 1994-07-14, summer 1994, 1994-06..1994-08), or none
    --ask N[,N] keep the scene to ask the family: one open task/v1 line in the list videos
    --asked, --answered the scenes kept to ask, or those you confirmed, to answer or correct them
    --top N the first N scenes (a place's option too)

    The lock is the sitting's, the same for every queue: the queue is read and you are asked with no lock held, and each answer's lines are appended under the record's writer lock.

    Ideas from calls

    A call's synthesis (logbook transcript synthesize, transcript synthesis) may propose up to three ideas the call put forward: a new one, or a refinement of an idea you named, by its id. They are proposals and nothing more. review idea lists them after the observed ones, marked [call], with the call and the utterances they rest on:

    $ logbook review idea
2 proposals · 0 observed ideas not yet placed, 0 placed · tier 3
  1  [call]  “Fjord Kiosk” (call “Yard plans” 2026-06-10; U4) → Fjord Kiosk, refines: The kiosk sells mooring lines too.
  2  [call]  “Shared haul-out calendar” (call “Yard plans” 2026-06-10; U8) → ____: One calendar the yard and the owners share.
nothing written: `review idea --accept N[,N]`, `--name N "…"`, `--reject N[,N]`, or `--all-above SCORE`

    --all-above never takes a call's idea: it has no score.

    Nothing reaches the Ideas shelf until you say yes here (or on the Ideas page of logbook serve, which reads the same queue). --accept (or --name N "…" for a new one) writes one entity/v1 line: a new idea, minted as any other, or, for a refinement, a line of the idea's own id. That line is tier 2 and never cites a tier-3 line, and a Granola call is tier 3: so of such a call it carries the name you accepted and nothing else, as an observed title of a tier-3 line is accepted; of a call at tier 2 or below it cites the call and keeps the words (extra.pitch for a new idea, extra.refines for a refinement). --reject writes the entity-review/v1 no as for any idea, on the idea's title for a new one and on the call against the idea for a refinement, at the call's tier: it is never proposed again; two calls that put the same new idea forward are one candidate that cites both. A home video's soundtrack is no call and proposes nothing; a synthesis the dream entry withholds today proposes nothing either, and comes back when the entry allows it again.

    Goals, once a week

    review goal asks each of your goals once a week: every standing goal open today (begun, not closed by a verdict, not past its until) that has no review dated this ISO week, of any period. You answer with the verdict, one of the five of goals: advancing, stalled, done, let-go or superseded, and your words when you want them.

    $ logbook review goal
2 goals to review this week (2026-W42) · 1 reviewed · tier 2
  1  #2  Sail to Lofoten and back between June and August, at anchor more nights than not.  (year) · last: advancing on 2026-10-08
  2  #3  Rebuild the engine over the winter.  (quarter) · never reviewed
nothing written: `review goal --say N advancing|stalled|done|let-go|superseded`, `--note N "…"`
$ logbook review goal --say 1 stalled --say 2 advancing --note 1 "The boat is on land."
stalled 1 “Sail to Lofoten and back between June and August, at anchor more nights than not.”: review/v1 (seq 7)
advancing 2 “Rebuild the engine over the winter.”: review/v1 (seq 8)

    Each verdict is written by the writer goal review uses, for the period week, at now: the line is the one goal review N stalled --period week writes, byte for byte (RFC 0036 amendment 1). A goal answered this week is not asked again until the next; a second verdict on the same day for the same period writes nothing and says so. Every number and every word is checked before the first line: a number the queue does not have, a sixth word, a goal said twice, or a --note on a goal not said exits 2 and writes nothing. A goal has no score, no no and no name of its own, so --accept, --reject, --name and --all-above are refused for it; --say and --note are refused for every other kind, as a place's and the speakers' options are. On a terminal each goal is asked in turn.

    The table

    What the sources saw is one derived table in the index, observed_entities in index.sqlite: a row per observed person, org, idea, asset and place, keyed by the kind and its strongest identifier, with the names seen, the identifiers as the lines wrote them, counts per source and per line kind, the days, first and last seen, the tier of the evidence, a few line ids, the canonical people seen on its lines, the org's hint and the canonical entity it resolves to. It is stamped per kind with the head and the settings it was built under, served while they stand and built again when they move; its shape is checked with PRAGMA table_info. observed_people and observed_places, the tables people review and places review read until 0.7, are views over it for one release. Nothing of it is in the record.

    What happened to a place's no

    Until 0.7 a place you rejected was kept in state/places-review.json, never as a line, and a backup left it out. It is a line now (RFC 0037, decision 3): setup places review --reject, review place --reject and the Places page of logbook serve each write one entity-review/v1 line of type place, and the file is a cache rebuilt from the lines, never the truth. The pairs the file held from before still count; setup places review --migrate-rejects (or review place --migrate-rejects) writes each once as an entity-review/v1 line, at the moment you said it, and a second run writes nothing. Until you run it, review says how many pairs are not lines yet.
