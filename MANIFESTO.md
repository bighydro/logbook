# A life is an adventure. An adventure gets logged.

Every ship keeps a log. Not for the owner's vanity — because the sea does not forgive forgetting, and because one day someone will ask where you were, who was aboard, and what the weather did. The log is the oldest honest record there is: written as it happens, never rewritten, kept by the person it is about.

Your life is already being logged. Your phone knows where you slept, your photos know who was there, your calendar knows what you meant to do, your messages know what you said. A dozen companies each keep a page of it, and none of them keeps it for you. You have never been handed the book.

Logbook is the book.

One line for each thing that happened, each chained to the one before, so nothing can be quietly rewritten. All of it in a folder you hold, in plain text, readable without us, in twenty years, in any editor. It writes itself. You read it back as days: where you were, who was with you, what you promised, how the year actually went. Not what you posted. What happened.

It is the opposite of a feed. No audience, no score, no followers, no streaks. There is a page, and the people who were on it. You hand a page to someone who was there, and they hand one back. That is the whole social network.

We built it because a life is an adventure, and an adventure only counts if someone keeps the log. And because once the keeping is done for you, you can stop keeping score and be where you are.

The format is free and belongs to no one. The specification is public domain; two independent implementations must agree before a version is final. There is no company behind it. You can leave at any time, with everything. That is what owning means.

Record it. Recount it. Hand a page to the people who were there.
